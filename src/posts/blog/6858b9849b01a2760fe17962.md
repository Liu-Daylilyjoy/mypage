# Getting Started with Pixel Art

>Pixel art is a form of digital art where images are created and edited at the pixel level. It has a nostalgic charm reminiscent of early video games and remains popular for its simplicity and expressive style. Whether you want to create retro game graphics, icons, or just enjoy a new art form, pixel art is a great skill to learn.

In this blog, we'll cover everything you need to get started with pixel art — from the basics and tools to tips and exercises.

---

## What is Pixel Art?

Pixel art is an art style created by arranging individual pixels (the smallest units of a digital image) to form characters, objects, and scenes. Unlike high-resolution digital paintings, pixel art embraces the blocky, grid-based aesthetic that highlights each pixel’s presence.

### Why Pixel Art?

- **Simplicity:** You can create compelling art with a small canvas and limited colors.
- **Nostalgia:** It evokes the look and feel of classic 8-bit and 16-bit games.
- **Accessibility:** It requires fewer resources and less processing power.
- **Creativity:** Working within constraints can boost your creativity.

## Basic Concepts

### 1. Canvas Size

Pixel art usually works on small canvases, like 16x16, 32x32, or 64x64 pixels. Starting small helps you focus on the overall shape and color placement.

### 2. Grid and Zoom

Working pixel by pixel requires zooming in (often 800% or more) and enabling grid view to see each pixel clearly.

### 3. Colors and Palette

Choose a limited color palette. Classic pixel art often uses 8 to 16 colors. Limiting colors helps maintain consistency and style.

### 4. Outlines and Shapes

Use outlines to define shapes clearly. Experiment with black or colored outlines depending on your style.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Create Your First Pixel Art

### Step 1: Choose Your Subject

Start simple: a smiley face, a heart, or a small character.

### Step 2: Set Up Your Canvas

- Open your pixel art tool.
- Create a new file with a small canvas, e.g., 32x32 pixels.
- Zoom in so you can see individual pixels.

### Step 3: Sketch the Outline

- Use a pencil tool set to 1 pixel size.
- Draw the basic shape or silhouette of your subject.
- Don’t worry about details yet.

### Step 4: Fill in Base Colors

- Choose the main colors.
- Fill your shape using the fill bucket or pencil tool.

### Step 5: Add Shading and Highlights

- Pick darker shades to add depth.
- Add lighter pixels for highlights.
- Keep shading simple and consistent with your light source.

### Step 6: Clean Up and Polish

- Remove stray pixels.
- Adjust outlines if necessary.
- Make sure the art looks good both zoomed in and at normal size.

## Tips for Beginners

- **Practice regularly:** Pixel art improves with practice.
- **Study other pixel art:** Analyze games and artists you like.
- **Work with layers:** If your tool supports layers, use them for easier editing.
- **Use symmetry:** Many pixel art subjects are symmetrical, so mirror your work.
- **Avoid anti-aliasing at first:** It smooths edges but can complicate pixel clarity.
- **Keep your palette small:** This helps unify your art style.

---

## Exercises to Try

1. **Create a 16x16 character sprite:** Design a simple character with front and side views.
2. **Make an animated pixel art:** A blinking eye or walking cycle.
3. **Pixel art icons:** Create icons like a heart, sword, or potion.
4. **Remake classic game sprites:** Try replicating Mario, Pac-Man, or other retro characters.
